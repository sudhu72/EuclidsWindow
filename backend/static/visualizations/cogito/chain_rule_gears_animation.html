Blame flowing backward through the whole 3-layer network: fan-out at the output, fan-in at layer A. Drag "output" and watch every gear respond.

drag to change "output" hypothetically — A1, A2, B1, B2's forward values stay fixed (they only depend on x1, x2 and upstream weights); their BLAME values ripple backward live.

Fan-out, at the output: blame_output doesn't just drive one gear backward — it drives BOTH B1 and B2, through two different ratios (w_C1 = 0.6 and w_C2 = -0.4). Watch both connections leaving the "output" gear light up together — one blame number, two separate downstream jobs.

Fan-in, at layer A: A1 doesn't just receive blame from one place — B1 and B2 BOTH feed into A1 (and both feed into A2 too), so A1's blame is the SUM of two separate contributions: blame_A1 = blame_B1×w_B1[0] + blame_B2×w_B2[0]. The gear for A1 still just shows one final rotation — because addition already happened before the gear ever turns — but the readout panel below shows both halves of that sum explicitly.

Why do A1, A2, B1, B2's gears spin at all, if their forward values are fixed? Because their gear rotation here represents their BLAME value, not their forward value — the thing that's actually flowing backward and changing as you move the slider. Only the output/e/loss gears (right side) represent forward-pass values turning; once you're purely in backward-only territory, the gears represent blame.
