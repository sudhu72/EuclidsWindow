Target encoding: each city becomes its average y, pulled toward the prior

The twelve rows from the tutorial. encoding = (sum of y + m × prior) / (n + m), prior = overall mean of y. m is the number of imaginary "average" rows each city starts with.

Out-of-fold: no row is allowed to see its own label

Same twelve rows, m = 2. Three folds: rows 1–4, 5–8, 9–12. To encode a fold, use only the other two folds' rows. A city missing from the other folds gets the prior.

Target-encoding pure noise: a fake AUC of 0.874

2,000 rows, a random user-ID column (1,000 possible IDs, 859 appear), coin-flip labels independent of the ID. Logistic regression on the target-encoded ID. Honest answer: AUC ≈ 0.5. Measured with scikit-learn 1.7.2 (TargetEncoder, LogisticRegression, 5-fold stratified CV).

Frequency encoding: each city becomes how common it is

No labels are used, so there is nothing to leak. The blind spot: cities with equal counts become identical.

The hashing trick: column = |hash| mod k

This page runs MurmurHash3 (32-bit, seed 0), the same hash scikit-learn's FeatureHasher uses, on each string's UTF-8 bytes. Colliding cities share a colour.
