Turning Angles Into Numbers

Fixed ratios born from similar triangles, a circle that frees them from ever needing a triangle again, a natural angle unit measured by the circle's own arc, two coordinate systems solving the same problem two different ways, a wave that's just a circle viewed from the side, and two rotations landing exactly where one combined rotation would. Each one built from a picture, not asserted.

Each panel below reads top to bottom: what's happening, a Try this to poke at, and why it matters.

Sine and Cosine, Freed From Triangles

What's happening: the point's x-coordinate is cos θ; its y-coordinate is sin θ. Drag the angle past 90° and watch the definitions keep working seamlessly, with no special case.

Try this: push θ past 180° and 270° — sin and cos never break, they just keep reading off the point's coordinates, exactly as the definition promises.

35°
Why it matters: the unit circle's own equation, x²+y²=1, becomes sin²θ+cos²θ=1 the moment you substitute — the Pythagorean identity, essentially for free.

Two Maps, One Point

What's happening: the same point, described two ways — how far right and up (Cartesian), or how far out and at what angle (polar). Drag either representation and watch the other update live.

A Wave Is a Circle, Viewed From the Side

What's happening: the point traces the unit circle on the left. Its height and horizontal position, plotted against the angle swept so far, draw out the sin and cos waves on the right — live.

Try this: press play and watch the Ferris-wheel point go around while the waves draw themselves out in perfect sync — the wave never stops being the circle, just viewed differently.

Why it matters: tan's vertical asymptote at 90° is just sin/cos with cos sliding through zero — watch it happen on the same plot.

Two Rotations, or One — Same Destination

What's happening: rotate a point by θ₁, then rotate the result by θ₂ more (gold path) — or rotate directly by θ₁+θ₂ in one step (teal point). Watch them land on the exact same spot, with both routes' coordinates computed and compared.

Try this: set any two angles and confirm the two-step gold path and the one-step teal point always coincide — that agreement is the angle-sum identity, made visible instead of memorized.
