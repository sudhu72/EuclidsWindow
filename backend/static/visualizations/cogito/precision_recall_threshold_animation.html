Why can't accuracy alone be trusted? If only 4 of these 10 emails are truly spam, a classifier that flags NOTHING as spam still gets 60% "accuracy" for free — technically correct most of the time, completely useless at its actual job. Precision and recall specifically separate "how much can I trust a positive flag" from "how much of the real thing did I catch," so a lazy classifier can't hide behind an imbalanced dataset.

Why does F1 use the harmonic mean instead of a plain average? A plain average lets one great number hide one terrible number — precision 1.0 and recall 0.1 would average to a deceptively decent-looking 0.55. The harmonic mean 2·P·R/(P+R) is pulled down hard by whichever of the two is smaller, so a classifier can't get a good F1 score by being excellent at one and neglecting the other entirely.

Is there a single "correct" threshold? No — it depends entirely on which mistake costs more in your actual situation. A spam filter that occasionally lets one spam email through (low threshold, lower precision) is usually more tolerable than one that buries real emails in a spam folder (high threshold, lower recall). Fraud detection, medical screening, and content moderation each answer this tradeoff differently, on purpose.
