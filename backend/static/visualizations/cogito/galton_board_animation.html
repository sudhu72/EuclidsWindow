The Galton board

Every single bounce is a 50/50 coin flip. Watch what the coin flips build anyway.

balls dropped: 0

Each single bounce is genuinely as far from bell-shaped as a distribution gets — a coin flip is exactly 50/50, a flat, two-outcome distribution with no curve to it at all.

But a ball's FINAL bin is the sum of 12 independent bounces, not just one. There's only one way to bounce right all 12 times (landing at the far-right bin), and only one way to bounce left all 12 times — but there are far more ways to end up with, say, 6 rights and 6 lefts in some order, since those can happen in many different sequences. More possible sequences land you in the middle bins than the edge bins, purely from counting — and that counting fact alone is what sculpts the coin flips into a bell shape.

This is the Central Limit Theorem, felt rather than proven: add up enough small independent random effects, and their sum trends toward a bell curve, regardless of how unlike a bell curve any single effect was on its own. A person's height, built from hundreds of small independent genetic and environmental nudges, follows the exact same logic as this pegboard.
