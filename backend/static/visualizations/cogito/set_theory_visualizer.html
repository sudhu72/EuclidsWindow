|ℤ| = |ℕ|: Zig-Zagging Through the Integers

f(n) = n/2 if n is even, −(n+1)/2 if n is odd — every natural number lands on a distinct integer, positive and negative alike.

Why it matters: ℤ looks twice the size of ℕ at a glance — this bijection is the exact proof that it isn't.

Enumerating the Rationals, One Diagonal at a Time

Every positive fraction p/q, arranged in a grid, walked along diagonals of constant p+q — skipping any fraction not already in lowest terms.

Why it matters: if this enumeration never repeats and never permanently skips any positive rational, every rational eventually gets its own natural-number position — exactly what "countably infinite" means.

Injective? Surjective? Bijective? Draw It and Find Out

4 domain dots on the left, always. Click a left dot, then click a right dot, to draw an arrow — click the same left dot again to cancel. Every left dot needs exactly one arrow before a function is fully defined.

Why it matters: a red-ringed dot means two arrows landed on it — a collision, breaking injective. A gray dot with no incoming arrow breaks surjective. At equal codomain size, watch these two failures always arrive together — never one without the other.

Relations, Equivalence Classes, and Union-Find

A relation is a grid of pairs: a filled cell in row a, column b means a ~ b. Reflexive = the whole diagonal is filled; symmetric = the grid is its own mirror image across the diagonal; transitive = a ~ b and b ~ c always force a ~ c. Every preset plays itself; use Step, Play, Jump to end or Reset.
