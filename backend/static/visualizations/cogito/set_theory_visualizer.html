Four Operations, and Two Laws Checked Live

Universe U = {1..10}. A = {1,2,3,4,5}, B = {4,5,6,7,8}. Pick an operation and watch the matching region light up.

Why it matters: every region on this diagram is a set; every button press is one of Part 1's four operations, and the two De Morgan buttons show both sides of the law landing on the identical shaded region, not just asserting they match.

Enumerating the Rationals, One Diagonal at a Time

Every positive fraction p/q, arranged in a grid, walked along diagonals of constant p+q — skipping any fraction not already in lowest terms.

Why it matters: if this enumeration never repeats and never permanently skips any positive rational, every rational eventually gets its own natural-number position — exactly what "countably infinite" means.

Build a Number Guaranteed to Be Missing

Five numbers below (edit them, or add your own line, then rebuild). Watch the diagonal digit get taken from each row, changed, and assembled into a new number that can't match any row.

Why it matters: this construction never looks at anything except each row's own digit at its own matching position — which is exactly why it works no matter what the list contains, or how long it is.
