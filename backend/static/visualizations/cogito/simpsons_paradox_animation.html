Simpson's Paradox — the trend that flips

Identical data. Toggle the view and watch which treatment "wins" reverse completely.

The raw numbers, in full: Mild cases — Treatment A: 9/10 recover (90%), Treatment B: 80/100 recover (80%). Severe cases — Treatment A: 30/100 recover (30%), Treatment B: 2/10 recover (20%). Treatment A wins by 10 points in both subgroups.

The hidden mechanism: an uneven split, not random chance. Treatment A was given to 100 severe patients but only 10 mild ones — mostly the hard cases, where success rates are low for everyone. Treatment B was given to 100 mild patients but only 10 severe ones — mostly the easy cases, where success rates are high for everyone. Combine the groups, and you're not really comparing "Treatment A vs Treatment B" anymore — you're comparing "mostly hard cases" against "mostly easy cases," with the treatment labels riding along almost as a coincidence.

The only way to catch this: look at the grouped view before trusting the combined one — exactly what the toggle above lets you do, and exactly what a single "overall success rate" table would never show you on its own.
