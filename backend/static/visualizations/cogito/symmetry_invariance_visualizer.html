What Refuses to Change

Three hands-on demonstrations of the master move in mathematics and physics: ignore what's moving and hunt for the one thing standing still. A parity invariant that proves the impossible, a conservation law you can watch, and a symmetry that breaks before your eyes.

The Invariant That Proves the Impossible

Every domino covers exactly one light and one dark square — an invariant no placement can break. So remove some squares, try to tile the rest, and watch the light-vs-dark counter decide the fate of your board before you place a single piece.

A matched light/dark count is necessary for a tiling to exist (not always sufficient) — but a mismatch is a rock-solid proof of impossibility. That one-directional certainty is the whole power of an invariant.

A Conservation Law You Can Watch

A planet orbits under a force aimed straight at the Sun — a setup with no preferred direction (perfect rotational symmetry). Noether's theorem says angular momentum must be conserved, and you can see it: the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times. Break the symmetry and watch the law fail.

Rotational symmetry ⟺ conserved angular momentum ⟺ equal areas in equal times. The three are one fact. Break the symmetry (add a preferred direction to space) and all three fail together.

Perfectly Balanced — Until It Isn't

A ball rests at the exact peak of a symmetric "Mexican-hat" landscape. Nothing prefers left or right. Yet it cannot stay: the tiniest fluctuation sends it into one of two identical valleys — a different one each time. The laws stay symmetric; the outcome never does.

This is spontaneous symmetry breaking — the same mechanism behind a cooling magnet choosing a north pole, the Higgs field giving particles mass, and matter winning out over antimatter so that anything exists at all. Structure is broken symmetry. Hit reset a few times and note the choice is never predictable.
