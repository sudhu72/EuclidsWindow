Four tabs, matching the tutorial's four verified constructions: encoding a sentence as a number, watching self-reference explode or resolve depending on one word, seeing why consistency, completeness, and decidability can't all survive together, and why no system can vouch for its own consistency.

Why it matters: every whole number has exactly one prime factorization, so decoding the Gödel number recovers the exact original symbol sequence, in the exact original order, with zero ambiguity -- a fact about numbers and a fact about sentences become the same fact.

Same Construction, One Word Changed

What's happening: the diagonal lemma builds a sentence φ that says "I have property P" about its own Gödel number, for any expressible property P. Pick a property and watch what happens when φ is evaluated.

Pick a property, then click Evaluate.
The one-word substitution: "false" and "true" are exhaustive opposites -- refusing both is a contradiction. "Provable-by-S" and "unprovable-by-S" are not that kind of opposite -- a sentence can be perfectly true while one fixed rulebook simply fails to reach it. That third option is the entire theorem.

Consistency, Completeness, Decidability -- Pick How Many

What's happening: Hilbert wanted all three, at once, for one fixed axiom system rich enough for arithmetic. Pick a real formal system and see which corners it actually holds onto.

Why it matters: only systems too weak to encode arithmetic (propositional logic, Presburger arithmetic -- no multiplication of variables) can ever hold all three corners at once. The moment a system is rich enough to encode its own provability (full Peano arithmetic), completeness and decidability both fail, forever, for any choice of axioms.

Can a System Vouch for Itself?

What's happening: click through the four steps of the second incompleteness theorem, one at a time, and watch the contradiction assemble.

Why it matters: this is why the first theorem's blind spot isn't just "one weird sentence" -- it's specifically the question of self-trust, and no consistent system rich enough for arithmetic can ever certify its own consistency from the inside.
