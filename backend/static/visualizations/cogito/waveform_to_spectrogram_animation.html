From waveform to spectrogram

Watch a small time-window slide across the wave while a spectrogram builds up beside it, column by column.

spectrogram building up (time → across, frequency ↑, brightness = strength)

A steady tone has the same pitch the whole way through, so every time-window's Fourier Transform reports strength concentrated at the same single frequency — column after column lights up at the same height, producing one flat horizontal band the whole way across.

A rising chirp's pitch increases continuously, so each successive time-window is centered on a slightly higher frequency than the one before it. Plot each window's peak frequency as you move left to right, and the bright spot climbs steadily upward — a straight diagonal line is exactly what "pitch rising at a constant rate over time" looks like once frequency is placed on its own axis.

Nothing about the Fourier Transform "knows" about chirps or tones specifically — it only ever answers "how much of each frequency is present in this one window." The shape you see is just that same honest answer, asked and plotted many times in a row.
