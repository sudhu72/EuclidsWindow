Scientific Notation in 64 Switches

Each bit is a switch with a lamp: lit means 1. Red-edged lamps are the sign, teal the exponent, purple the mantissa. Every example is a one-click preset that plays itself; use Step to go one move at a time, Play to watch, or Jump to end for the answer. Every number is computed live by this page's own 64-bit arithmetic, which is the same IEEE 754 arithmetic Python and NumPy use. There's nothing to type.

How a double is built: sign, exponent, mantissa

value = (−1)sign × 1.mantissa × 2(exponent field − 1023). The leading 1. is never stored (the "hidden bit"). Exponent fields 0 and 2047 are reserved for zero, subnormals, infinity and NaN.

Every input is rounded to a tick, and so is the answer

The page adds the exact stored values with whole-number arithmetic, then shows the two doubles (ticks) the true sum falls between and which one IEEE 754 picks: the nearest, with ties going to the tick whose last bit is 0.

Machine epsilon: the gap between 1 and the next double

2−52 ≈ 2.22 × 10−16. Anything smaller than half a tick vanishes when added; above 253 the ticks are 2 apart.

The edges of the range

Past the top: infinity. Below the bottom: subnormals, then zero. Questions with no answer: NaN, which isn't even equal to itself.

Catastrophic cancellation: subtracting nearly equal numbers

The subtraction is exact, but it exposes the rounding errors already in its inputs. The fix is always a rearrangement that avoids the subtraction.

Softmax and log-sum-exp, naive and stable

Subtracting the largest score from every score changes nothing mathematically (it cancels top and bottom), but makes the biggest exponent e0 = 1: no overflow, and a sum of at least 1.

Multiply probabilities, or add their logs

The probability of n fair coin flips all landing as observed is 0.5n. Watch the product fall off the bottom of the range while the sum of logs stays ordinary.

float32, float16 and bfloat16 side by side

float16 is 1 / 5 / 10 bits: precise but tiny range (max 65,504). bfloat16 is 1 / 8 / 7: float32's range, about 2–3 digits. Each value is rounded to the nearest number the format can hold.

Kahan summation: keep the change

y = x − c; t = total + y; c = (t − total) − y; total = t. The variable c holds (minus) whatever the last addition rounded away, and adds it back next time.
