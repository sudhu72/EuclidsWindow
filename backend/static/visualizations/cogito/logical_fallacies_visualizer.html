The Estimator That's Biased Even Though the Coin Isn't

What's happening: for EVERY possible sequence of n fair flips, compute that one sequence's own empirical "P(heads | right after a k-streak)," then average that number across every sequence containing the streak at least once. The coin's true probability is exactly 0.5 on every single flip — watch how far the average of this natural estimator drifts from 0.5 anyway, and how that gap shrinks as n grows.

A Rare Match Isn't the Same as a Sure Thing

What's happening: a trait matches only 1-in-N people if they're innocent. Assume exactly one truly guilty match exists in the whole population. Drag the sliders and watch how few of the "matches" are actually guilty — even when the trait sounds damning.

The fallacious reading treats "1 in N, if innocent" as if it were "P(guilty)" directly. The correct number instead asks: of everyone who matches (guilty and innocent), what share is actually guilty?
