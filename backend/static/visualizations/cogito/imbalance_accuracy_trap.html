Accuracy only counts how often you're right, not what you were right about. A model that always says "not fraud" is automatically correct on every single non-fraud transaction — so the rarer fraud actually is, the more of the dataset that model gets right by doing nothing at all.

Precision and recall don't have this blind spot. The "does nothing" model's recall is always exactly 0% — it never finds a single fraud case, no matter how rare fraud is — and that number stays honestly damning at every fraud rate on the slider, precisely because recall only asks about the class that actually matters here.

The real Kaggle credit card fraud dataset sits at roughly the far-left end of this slider — under 1% fraud — which is exactly the range where accuracy is at its most dangerously reassuring, and exactly why Part 2 of the tutorial insists on precision, recall, and PR-AUC instead.
