Why does training loss keep falling while validation loss eventually rises? Training loss measures fit to data the model is actively memorizing against. Given enough epochs, a flexible enough model can start fitting the training set's specific noise and quirks — not the general pattern anymore, just that exact data (Part 1.5's overfitting). Validation loss, measured on data the model never trains on, has no reason to keep improving once the model starts memorizing noise instead of learning signal — that's the moment the two curves visibly diverge.

So the real stopping rule: keep training only as long as validation loss keeps improving; stop once it starts consistently getting worse. The best moment to have stopped is exactly the validation curve's minimum — every epoch after that is the model getting quietly worse at generalizing, even while it keeps looking better and better on the data it's actively studying.

In practice, "patience" is added — wait a handful of epochs after validation loss stops improving before actually stopping, since a single bad epoch could just be noise rather than the real turning point. Other real stopping rules besides early stopping: a fixed epoch budget decided in advance, a convergence threshold (stop once the loss barely changes step to step — Part 1.3's hillside has gone essentially flat), or simply running out of compute budget.
