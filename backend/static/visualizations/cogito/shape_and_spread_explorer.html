Why it matters

The histogram shows the raw shape. The box plot compresses it to five numbers plus outlier flags — fast to compare across groups, but blind to bimodality (a box plot of a two-humped distribution just looks like one wide box). The violin plot fixes exactly that blind spot by showing the full density instead of five numbers. The Q-Q plot answers a sharper, different question: not "what shape is this" but "does this shape match a normal distribution specifically" — points hugging the diagonal say yes; a curve bending away says no, and the direction of the bend says which of this file's shapes you're actually looking at.
