How Do You Prove a List Is Finished?

Three tabs, matching the tutorial's three verified demonstrations: a finite, provable classification (Platonic solids), a law of nature proven the same way (crystallographic restriction), and a case where exhaustiveness is provably impossible (diagonalization).

Angle-Defect Search

What's happening: pick a face shape (p-gon) and how many meet at each corner (q). The wedge diagram unfolds that corner flat — green means it folds into a real 3D point, gray means it lies perfectly flat (tiles a plane, no solid), red means the faces would have to overlap.

Why it matters: exactly 5 of the 30 cells above ever turn green, no matter how far the grid is extended — a finite, checkable search, not a lucky absence of counterexamples.

The exception that proves the rule: n=5 fails here -- and real quasicrystals do show 5-fold symmetry, but only because their structure never exactly repeats (non-periodic), which is exactly the one assumption this proof depends on.

No Oracle Can Ever Be Right About This Program

What's happening: suppose a perfect oracle H predicts whether a program halts or loops forever. Program D asks H what it predicts about D itself, then deliberately does the opposite. Pick a prediction for H to make, and watch D immediately falsify it.

Pick a prediction above.
The Gödel connection: replace "halts" with "provable" and "D" with a self-referential arithmetic sentence G, and this exact contradiction is Gödel's own proof -- G is true precisely because a consistent system can never prove it.
