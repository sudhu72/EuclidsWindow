The mean adds every value together and divides. If one value grows without bound, the sum grows right along with it, and so does the mean — there is no ceiling on how far one extreme point can drag it.

The median only cares about sorted position, never magnitude. Whether the largest value in this dataset is 56 or 5,000,000, it's still just "the largest value" — the middle of nine sorted numbers doesn't shift just because the top one got more extreme, as long as it stays the top one. This is exactly why the median is called a robust statistic: a small number of extreme values can't hijack it.

The IQR outlier flag above uses Q3 + 1.5 × IQR as the upper cutoff (Part 5 of the tutorial) — built from Q1 and Q3, not from the mean, which is exactly why the flag stays trustworthy even while X is actively distorting the mean.
