A double integral is a volume, and you can slice it either way

Columns: f(x, y) = x·y² over 0 ≤ x ≤ 2, 0 ≤ y ≤ 3 (exact volume 18). Fubini: the same volume as a stack of slices, in either order. Triangle: ∫₀¹∫ₓ¹ e^(y²) dy dx, impossible one way, one line the other way.

Polar coordinates and ∫e^(−x²)dx = √π

A thin ring at radius r has area 2πr·dr: the Jacobian's r. Adding rings of height e^(−r²) builds the volume of the round Gaussian hill, which is I², the square of the one-dimensional bell-curve area.
