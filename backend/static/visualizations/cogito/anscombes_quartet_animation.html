Anscombe's Quartet — same stats, different realities

Four datasets. Nearly identical mean, variance, correlation, and best-fit line. Look at what those numbers hid.

Linear regression and the correlation coefficient only ever measure one specific thing: how well a straight line fits, and how strong a LINEAR relationship is. Neither one has any way to notice a curve (Dataset II), a single influential outlier (Dataset III), or a relationship built almost entirely by one far-away point (Dataset IV) — because none of those situations are inherently linear-relationship questions. The math did exactly what it was asked; the question itself just wasn't broad enough to catch what was actually going on.

This is the entire, permanent case for "always plot your data" before trusting any summary statistic — not as a platitude, but because Francis Anscombe specifically constructed these four datasets in 1973 to prove, with real numbers, that a summary table alone cannot distinguish four genuinely different situations that demand four genuinely different responses.
