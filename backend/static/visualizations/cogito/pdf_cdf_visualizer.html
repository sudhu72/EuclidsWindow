PDFs and CDFs, Made Visible

Six verified demos: watch a density's area-so-far trace a live CDF; build uniform and exponential distributions from their sliders; wait for the k-th event with the Gamma distribution (and meet chi-square and Gamma–Poisson conjugacy); watch the normal distribution's CDF come from the error function, not a shortcut; read every moment off one function and turn sums into products with moment generating functions; and watch inverse-CDF sampling turn plain uniform randomness into any shape you like.

Waiting for the k-th Event

What's happening: the Gamma distribution is the wait until the k-th event of a steady random stream (rate λ per minute): the sum of k independent exponential gaps. Shape α = k sets the curve's form; rate λ only stretches the time axis. The same family is the chi-square distribution, and the conjugate prior for a Poisson rate.

Try this: every button replays one worked example from Part 3b, with every number computed live. Start with Third call, then compare it to k = 1 is the exponential. The sliders let you explore your own shape and rate afterwards.

Moment Generating Functions: Every Moment in One Function

What's happening: the MGF is M(t) = E[e^(tX)]. Its slope at t = 0 is the mean, its second derivative there is E[X²], and so on. The trick that makes it famous: for independent pieces, the MGF of a sum is the product of their MGFs, so sums of random variables become multiplication.

Try this: start with Exponential and read the slope (2) and curvature (8) off the curve at t = 0, the mean and E[X²] from Part 3. Then Sum of two normals: 200,000 simulated sums land exactly on the Normal(15, 5²) curve the MGF product predicts. Finish with Chernoff to see the bound from 03 · Probability §3.6 as the lowest point of a curve.
